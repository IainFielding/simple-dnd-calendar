# The Moons

D&D 5e's calendars give the days, months, festivals and seasons of each world, but no moons. Simple D&D Calendar adds them: each of D&D 5e's calendars has its world's moons, worked out from a period and one moment when each moon's phase is known. From that, the calendar knows every moon's phase at any moment, past or future, and the same date always shows the same sky.

This page covers how the moons move, and the moons of each calendar.

## How the moons move

### Phases

Each moon goes from new to full and back to new once a **period**, the days from one new moon to the next. Its phase is named for whichever eighth of the cycle it is in:

New Moon → Waxing Crescent → First Quarter → Waxing Gibbous → Full Moon → Waning Gibbous → Last Quarter → Waning Crescent

Each name is centred on its moment, so a moon is called "Full Moon" from a sixteenth of its period before full until a sixteenth after.

### In the sky

The moons travel the bowl above the day wheel, following the sun but falling behind it by their phase:

| Phase | Highest in the sky |
| --- | --- |
| New Moon | Noon, close beside the sun, its dark face turned to you |
| First Quarter | 18:00 |
| Full Moon | Midnight, opposite the sun |
| Last Quarter | 06:00 |

A moon is drawn while it is above the horizon and high enough to clear the bar, lit on the side the sun is on: from the right as it waxes, from the left as it wanes. Hover over a moon for its name and phase.

### Full and new moons

A moon is full on the calendar day that its phase passes full, from one midnight to the next, and new on the day it passes new. Those days are marked on the calendar with a moon beside the date, whose tooltip says which moons are full or new. When more than one moon is full on the same day, they are listed as full together, as Luna and Celene are.

The same events appear in the `/date` command's reply and, if the GM turns them on, in each dawn's announcement.

### The forecast

Hover over the seasonal timeline for each moon's phase and when it is next full, in days and as a date.

### Moonlight

With **Darken Scenes at Night** on, the moons lighten the night. The brightest moon counts, by how much of its face is lit and how high it rides: a full moon at midnight lightens the scene most, a crescent low in the sky barely at all, and a moon below the horizon not at all.

## The calendars

### Calendar of Harptos (Forgotten Realms)

| Moon | Period | First full moon counted from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Selûne** | 30 days, 10½ hours | 06:00 on 30 Nightal 1500 DR |

Selûne's period is the one given in Forgotten Realms lore, and the module takes her to have been full at the start of 1372 DR. Counted forward through every Shieldmeet since, that full moon comes round three-quarters of a day before 1501 DR, the year D&D 5e's Calendar of Harptos begins.

Selûne's period is exactly a twelfth of the Harptos year averaged over a Shieldmeet cycle: 48 turns take 1,461 days, which is four years, Shieldmeet included. So Selûne's phases fall on the same dates every four years. In 1501 DR, for instance, she is full on 30 Hammer, the day before Midwinter.

The Tears of Selûne, the glittering shards that follow her, trail behind her across the sky. They twinkle by their own light, however much of Selûne's face is lit, and fade away as the sky grows light.

### Calendar of Greyhawk (Oerth)

| Moon | Period | Full on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Luna**, the great moon | 28 days | Needfest 4, 576 CY |
| **Celene**, the little blue-green moon | 91 days, once a season | Needfest 4, 576 CY |

In the setting, Luna and Celene are full together on the fourth day of Needfest, every year, because Oerth's year is 364 days with four 7-day festival weeks. Both moons fit into that year exactly: Luna 13 times and Celene 4 times.

D&D 5e's Calendar of Greyhawk is 360 days, with festival weeks of 6 days. The module keeps the moons' periods from the setting, so they no longer fit the year. They are full together on Needfest 4 in 576 CY, D&D 5e's year zero for Greyhawk, and every 364 days after that, which is 4 days later on the calendar each year. Their shared full moon works its way through the whole year, skips one year as it wraps round, and is back on Needfest 4 after 91 years, in 667 CY. In 591 CY, for instance, they are full together on 2 Coldeven.

Each moon on its own also falls 4 days later each year: Luna's full moons drift through the months, as do Celene's.

### Calendar of Khorvaire (Eberron)

Eberron has twelve moons, one for each month of D&D 5e's Calendar of Khorvaire, which shares their names. *Eberron: Rising from the Last War* says each month is named for the moon whose orbit brings it closest to the world that month, so each moon is full in the middle of its own month in 998 YK, where D&D 5e's calendar begins. The book gives no periods, so the module makes each moon a week slower than the one before.

| Moon | Colour | Period | Full in 998 YK on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Zarantyr** | Pearly white | 28 days | 15 Zarantyr |
| **Olarune** | Pale orange | 35 days | 15 Olarune |
| **Therendor** | Pale grey | 42 days | 15 Therendor |
| **Eyre** | Silvery grey | 49 days | 15 Eyre |
| **Dravago** | Pale lavender | 56 days | 15 Dravago |
| **Nymm** | Pale yellow | 63 days | 15 Nymm |
| **Lharvion** | Dull white | 70 days | 15 Lharvion |
| **Barrakas** | Pale grey | 77 days | 15 Barrakas |
| **Rhaan** | Pale blue | 84 days | 15 Rhaan |
| **Sypheros** | Smoky grey, the Shadow Moon | 91 days | 15 Sypheros |
| **Aryth** | Orange-red | 98 days | 15 Aryth |
| **Vult** | Grey | 105 days | 15 Vult |

Zarantyr's period is exactly a month, so it is full on the 15th of every month, every year. The other moons' periods don't fit the month or the 336-day year, so after 998 YK their full moons drift away from their own months. Every one of the twelve is a whole number of weeks, so each moon's full moons all fall on the same day of the week. With twelve moons, few nights pass without at least one of them full or close to it.

### Gregorian

| Moon | Period | New moon counted from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Moon** | 29.530589 days, the average lunar month | 18:14 on 6 January 2000 |

This is Earth's Moon, counted from a real new moon. The real Moon's months vary in length, so a phase here can be up to about half a day from the real one, and the calendar has no time zones. It's close enough to put the full moons on the right nights.

### Other calendars

A calendar not listed here has no moons, until a module or world script adds some.

## Changing the moons

Modules and world scripts can change any calendar's moons, or give moons to a calendar of their own, in the `sogrom-simple-dnd-calendar.moons` hook. It fires once, during Foundry's `setup`, before the calendar is first drawn. It is passed the moons of every calendar, keyed by the calendar's value in `CONFIG.DND5E.calendar.calendars`, and the world's calendar.

Each moon has:

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| `id` | A unique key for the moon. |
| `name` | Its name, or a localization key for it. |
| `period` | Days from one new moon to the next. |
| `color` | The colour of its lit face, such as `"#eef1ff"`. |
| `epoch` | A moment its phase is known: `{ year, day, hour }`, with the year as the calendar shows it, the day of the year counted from 0, and the hour of that day. The hour may be negative, or more than a day's hours, to land between days. |
| `epochPhase` | Its phase at the epoch: `0` new, `0.25` first quarter, `0.5` full, `0.75` last quarter. By default `0`. |
| `trail` | Optional. `"tears"` draws a trail of glints behind it, as the Tears of Selûne follow her. |

For example, to give a homebrew calendar a red moon that was full at midnight starting its first day:

```js
Hooks.on("sogrom-simple-dnd-calendar.moons", moons => {
  moons.myCalendar = [
    { id: "blood", name: "The Blood Moon", period: 33, color: "#e05a4f", epoch: { year: 1, day: 0 }, epochPhase: 0.5 }
  ];
});
```

Or to slow Selûne to an even 30 days:

```js
Hooks.on("sogrom-simple-dnd-calendar.moons", moons => {
  moons.harptos[0].period = 30;
});
```
